You can start with a fast model for simple work. You can validate the response. You can retry once with a tighter prompt. You can fall back to another provider if needed. You can escalate to a stronger model only when the task deserves it.

That is how you control cost without making the product feel unreliable.

The fourth principle is logging outcomes, not just requests.

A dashboard that only shows token usage is not enough. Track cost per successful task. Track retry rate. Track fallback rate. Track p95 latency. Track empty responses. Track invalid JSON. Track user corrections. Track whether a human had to step in.

Those are the metrics that tell you whether your model strategy is actually working.

And here is the important cultural shift: model orchestration is not only for big companies. Small teams need it even more, because they cannot afford to manually fix every weird edge case.

A simple orchestration ladder can be enough.

Step one: classify the task.

Step two: choose the cheapest reliable model tier.

Step three: validate the output.

Step four: retry or fallback based on the failure type.

Step five: escalate only when necessary.

Step six: log the result so tomorrow's routing decision gets smarter.
